# Generational Definition of Wealth

Wealth is not merely what a person possesses during their lifetime. Wealth is what they build, preserve, and successfully transfer so that future generations continue to benefit from it.

A person may appear extremely wealthy because they possess money, property, authority, or resources. But if those things collapse, disappear, or become useless after their death, their wealth was temporary and incomplete.

True wealth includes anything valuable that continues producing fruit beyond the original owner:

- Financial assets and productive property
- Wisdom, knowledge, and principles
- Faith and covenantal understanding
- Institutions, systems, and opportunities
- Reputation, influence, and social capital
- A stable family and prepared successors
- A legacy that enables future generations to flourish

This is why Moses can be viewed as wealthier than Solomon under a generational definition of wealth. Solomon possessed far more visible riches during his lifetime, but much of his kingdom fractured shortly after his death. Moses possessed less personally, yet the laws, structures, revelation, identity, and spiritual inheritance connected to his life continued benefiting generations long after him.

## Core Principle

True wealth is measured not only by what comes into your hands, but by what remains fruitful after it leaves your hands.

## Short Form

Wealth is preserved and transferable value that continues blessing future generations.

## Teaching Statement

The richest person is not necessarily the person who accumulates the most, but the person whose life creates the greatest enduring inheritance.
