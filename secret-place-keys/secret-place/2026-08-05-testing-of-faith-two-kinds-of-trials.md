# The Testing of Faith: Two Kinds of Trials

*Working through James 1:2–5, the Jesus model, and what it means to put your own flesh on trial.*

## It's not the event that's tested — it's the response

The various trials are opportunities to put your flesh to death. There are many trials, and you need patience because the next trial will always come — as long as we live in this fallen world, we will have fallen-world trials.

But here's the key: **your flesh is under trial. Your spirit is not.** Nothing can put your spirit on trial, because God is our judge, and we are already submitted to Him spiritually. But we need to be the judge of our own flesh — we need to take our flesh under trial and submit it to God and His patience.

So when the devil attacks — "oh, you're trying to attack my daughter" — okay, then I better be strapped up with preparation. And the way I prepare is by giving room for my faith to manifest and not my flesh, by continuously putting my flesh under trial *myself*, so that the testing of my faith produces patience — not so I can endure the devil's attacks longer, but so I can more easily trust God time after time, and trust Him more and more. The more faith, the more quickly and the bigger the mountains are moved. And I need patience because this is a lifelong thing — I need to get to the point where, on my deathbed, I am moving the biggest mountains quicker than I ever have before.

It's called *testing* of our faith because the trials are supposed to reveal what part of us has faith and what part is holding it back. If a little faith comes out, there's still the rest to reveal — so that all of it comes out, so the mountains move quicker, so the bigger ones actually get removed. A little faith moves small mountains; big mountains only move over a long period unless the faith is fully revealed.

**It's only called testing of faith because it's revealing the faith. The trial isn't putting your faith under pressure — it's putting your flesh under pressure, and that reveals the state of your faith.**

## Two kinds of trials

James says "various kinds of trials," and I believe there are two:

1. **The Jesus trial — putting your own flesh under trial on purpose.** This is what actually builds faith. This is prayer and fasting (Matthew 17:21) — voluntarily putting pressure on your own flesh. This is the trial that trains you.
2. **The world trial — when the world hits you.** For this one you need wisdom, and you need faith to ask for wisdom and believe you're walking in it. And where does that faith come from? From having already put your flesh under trial in the first kind. That's why he says *various kinds* of trials — they're not the same, and they work together.

## Voluntary subjection — the Jesus model

Jesus was never actually hit with worldly trials, because He decided to lay down His own life. Even Pilate came at Him with persecution and Jesus essentially said: *you're not putting me on trial — I lay down my life for my sheep. I put myself here. I put my own flesh under trial. You didn't do this to me.*

The goal is to be like Jesus: to end up where you end up not because external circumstances drove you there, but because you dictate where you go based on obedience to the Father. If you go somewhere that puts your flesh under pressure — because you chose it, because you were obedient to go there — then good. Because it means that when the world tries to do the same thing to you, it's now trying to touch your spirit instead, and it can't, because your flesh is already subject — because of the moments you put it under trial yourself.

## Why wisdom matters (v.5) — and where it actually comes from

Endurance without wisdom wastes the trial. If you endured while sinning or compromising, the flesh was strengthened, not killed. Wisdom (Sophia) is the God-given skill of knowing exactly how to respond rightly when flesh and spirit collide. There's no shame in failing or backing out of a trial — God is ready to give wisdom for it.

But here's the catch: **if you ask for wisdom out of your flesh, you won't get the right kind of wisdom.** Divine wisdom, Sophia, is what James says we need to navigate trials of the world. If we respond to fleshly world-trials with more flesh, and then ask for "wisdom" out of that same flesh, we're just living a fleshly life asking a fleshly question. The only way to actually receive divine wisdom is to be asking from your spirit — single-minded, not double-minded.

## Double-minded vs. single-minded

"Let not that man who doubts think he shall receive anything of the Lord — a double-minded man is unstable in all his ways."

**Double-minded** = identifying with both the flesh and the spirit.
**Single-minded** = identifying only with what your spirit is, not with what your flesh is.

The way out of double-mindedness is believing your true standing, no matter your external standing — "let the brother of low degree rejoice that he is exalted, but the rich that he is made low." That's the same move: stop identifying with the external/fleshly reading of your situation, and identify only with the spiritual reality.

## Did Jesus face internal temptation?

James 1:14 — "every man is tempted when he is drawn away of his own [desires] and enticed." Why does that have to mean sinful desires already living inside a person? If we're born again, we've been purified from that. It only becomes sin when you act wrongly on a desire — **thoughts are not sin.**

So if Jesus was tempted in every way just as we are (Hebrews 4:15), and we are tempted when drawn away by our own desires — but He had no negative desires of His own — then maybe on the mountaintop, in the 40 days of fasting and prayer, He took on every other man's desire Himself. Not that He carried sinful desire all His life, but that in that specific trial, something happened where He faced it fully — internal temptation, not just external — so that He could conquer it once and for all, and actually get rid of ours when we lay it at His feet.

If Jesus never faced internal temptation, I have no way out of internal temptation myself, because my Lord and Savior wouldn't have defeated it for me. Internal sin is just as real as external sin — He had to defeat it, or we couldn't.

## Synthesis

Trials are not merely hardships but moments of revelation that expose what is governing a person — flesh or spirit. The testing of faith is not the event itself, but the response produced under pressure. Endurance lets a person remain in the trial, but without wisdom the opportunity within it can be wasted. Wisdom is the God-given ability to respond correctly when the inner conflict is activated. As a person matures — especially through voluntary obedience that puts the flesh under pressure *beforehand* — their responses become increasingly governed by the Spirit rather than circumstance. This is what's seen perfectly in Jesus: He experienced real trials but was never ruled by them, because His life was fully aligned with the Father, and because He had already, voluntarily, put His own flesh under trial.

**You don't grow because trials happen. You grow because of how you respond when they do.**

The goal is not to control circumstances — it's to become ungoverned by them.
